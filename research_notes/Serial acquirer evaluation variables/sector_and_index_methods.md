# Sector-specific considerations for acquisition-driven companies, and how thematic/factor indexes select and weight constituents

Research date: 2026-11-12. All company figures are in each company's own reporting currency (CSU: USD; TOI: EUR; WSP: CAD; TFI: USD; Aecon: CAD) unless stated. Figures are quoted from the cited sources; nothing here is estimated. Where a figure came from a secondary summary rather than the company's own release, it is marked "(secondary)". No buy/sell views are given.

Verification caveat that applies throughout: the MSCI Quality methodology PDF (May 2025) downloaded but could not be text-extracted in this environment, so MSCI details are from search-result excerpts of the 2013/2014/2022/2025 documents, not a full read of the current version.

## Q1. For each of the four sectors, which metrics are most decisive for acquisitive companies, and which generic metrics mislead?

### Takeaway
Each company headlines a different, sector-specific "truth metric": organic growth and FCFA2S for CSU/Topicus, net revenue organic growth / adjusted EBITDA margin / backlog / DSO / net-debt-to-EBITDA for WSP, operating ratio / ROIC by segment / free cash flow for TFI, and backlog composition plus legacy fixed-price losses and margin "ex-legacy" for Aecon. IFRS net income and reported EPS are the most misleading generic metrics in all four sectors in 2025-26, because each company had a large non-operating item distorting them (Asseco fair-value charge at Topicus, derivative losses and acquisition amortisation at WSP, a CAD 128M fair-value charge at Aecon, net income down ~30% at CSU while FCFA2S rose 14%).

### Inferences
- A generic serial-acquirer scorecard maps onto the sectors roughly as follows (inference from what each company itself headlines):
  - "Organic growth" → CSU/TOI: FX-neutral organic and recurring/maintenance organic growth; WSP: net revenue organic growth (not gross revenue, which includes pass-through subconsultant costs); TFI: not a clean concept because volumes/pricing are cyclical, so compare across a cycle; Aecon: backlog and awards growth, with contract type.
  - "Profitability" → CSU/TOI: FCFA2S margin, not net income; WSP: adjusted EBITDA margin on net revenue; TFI: adjusted operating ratio by segment (inverse scale: lower is better, must be sign-flipped in any score); Aecon: adjusted EBITDA margin and margin ex-legacy.
  - "Returns on capital" → must include goodwill and acquired intangibles for all five; TFI discloses segment ROIC, CSU/TOI historically emphasise ROIC plus organic growth, WSP and Aecon do not headline ROIC.
  - "Leverage" → definitions differ (WSP excludes leases; TFI quotes funded debt to EBITDA; Aecon quotes net debt/TTM adjusted EBITDA; CSU/TOI have subsidiary-level non-recourse debt), so raw ratios are not like-for-like.
- Margin levels are not comparable across sectors (WSP ~19% adjusted EBITDA on net revenue, Aecon ~5% adjusted EBITDA, TFI ~8% operating margin implied by a 91.9% operating ratio). A cross-sector score should compare each company to its own sector norm or its own history, not rank raw margins.
- Single-year snapshots mislead for TFI (cyclical trough in 2025, sharp rebound in 2026) and Aecon (legacy losses rolling off); multi-year or through-cycle averages are more defensible.
- Point-in-time leverage misleads for WSP around large deals (0.9x at Dec 2025 → 2.3x at June 2026 with no deterioration in the business).

### Gaps
- No broker sector primer or CFA Institute text was retrieved; the "which metrics analysts treat as decisive" answer is built from the companies' own headline KPIs, trade press and analyst blogs rather than from a sell-side primer.
- No sourced full-year ROIC for TFI, WSP, Aecon, CSU or Topicus was found (only TFI Q4 2025 segment ROIC, secondary).
- No sourced Aecon split of backlog by contract type (fixed-price vs cost-plus/collaborative/recurring) was retrieved, though Aecon publishes one in its MD&A.
- WSP's FY2025 adjusted EBITDA margin percentage and 2025-2027 plan numeric targets were not retrieved.

## Q2. Acquisition track records and capital allocation of the five companies as of 2025-2026; is Aecon regarded as a serial acquirer?

### Takeaway
CSU and Topicus are archetypal programmatic serial acquirers; WSP is a platform acquirer doing progressively larger deals (POWER Engineers 2024, Ricardo Oct 2025, TRC Feb 2026) with an explicit 1.0-2.0x leverage band; TFI is a serial acquirer in a cyclical industry whose two largest US deals (UPS Freight 2021, Daseke 2024) have a mixed integration record and which is currently pausing large M&A. Aecon is an occasional bolt-on acquirer whose investment narrative is dominated by fixed-price legacy losses and backlog, and no source found treats it as a serial acquirer.

## Q3. How comparable are per-share metrics across reporting currencies and accounting definitions, and what normalisations do analysts apply?

### Takeaway
All five companies report under IFRS (the sources label their adjusted measures "non-IFRS"), so the comparability problem is less IFRS-vs-US-GAAP and more three other things: different reporting currencies (USD, EUR, CAD), company-defined non-IFRS measures with different add-backs (FCFA2S, adjusted EPS, adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow), and lease treatment in leverage ratios. Absolute per-share figures are not comparable; ratios, growth rates and yields computed within one currency are.

### Cited Findings
- Non-GAAP measures are not defined under IFRS, and company-chosen measures are used precisely to bridge the gap between statutory results and management's view of performance — [KPMG, IFRS vs US GAAP: non-GAAP measures](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2024/how-companies-communicate-financial-performance.html)
- IFRS 18 will require some management-defined performance measures to be disclosed and reconciled inside the financial statements; cash-flow-type measures such as free cash flow were proposed to fall outside that definition — [KPMG on IFRS 18 / primary financial statements](https://kpmg.com/us/en/articles/2023/proposed-changes-to-ifrs-standards.html)
- Basic/diluted EPS mechanics are similar but not identical under IFRS and US GAAP — [Deloitte DART, EPS roadmap Appendix A](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/presentation/asc260-10/roadmap-earnings-per-share/appendix-a-differences-between-us-gaap)
- IFRS permits capitalisation of development costs when criteria are met whereas US GAAP generally expenses R&D, which affects EBITDA comparisons with US peers — [Wall Street Prep, US GAAP vs IFRS](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/us-gaap-vs-ifrs-differences-similarities-examples-pdf-cheat-sheet/)
- TFI's adjusted measures are labelled non-IFRS and it reports in USD — [TFI Q2 2026 press release](https://tfiintl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/7/TFI-International-Press-Release-July-27-2026-EN.pdf)
- Lease treatment differs by company-defined metric: WSP's net debt excludes lease liabilities, and its TRC multiple was quoted on a pre-IFRS 16 basis — [WSP Q4 2025 release](https://www.wsp.com/en-gl/investors/press-releases/details/wsp-releases-strong-q4-2025-results-and-issues-2026-financial-outlook/3245102/2026); [Financier Worldwide](https://www.financierworldwide.com/fw-news/2025/12/18/wsp-global-strikes-33bn-trc-deal)
- CSU and Topicus report "organic growth" both as reported and FX-adjusted, and the two differ materially (CSU Q2 2026: 3% vs 1%) — [CSU Q2 2026 release](https://csisoftware.com/constellation-software-inc-announces-results-for-the-second-quarter-ended-june-30-2026-and-declares-quarterly-dividend/)
- Statutory vs adjusted gaps in a single period can be very large: Aecon Q2 2026 diluted EPS -CAD 1.58 vs adjusted +CAD 0.33; Topicus FY2025 net income -53% vs FCFA2S +23% — [MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/aecon-group-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-01/); [Topicus FY2025 release](https://mfn.se/all/a/topicus-com-inc/constellation-software-inc-and-topicus-com-inc-announce-results-for-topicus-com-inc-for-the-fourth-quarter-and-year-ended-december-31-2025-0857b411)

### Inferences
- Normalisations that follow from the above (standard practice, stated here as inference because no single primer was retrieved):
  1. Never compare absolute per-share values across companies; share counts are arbitrary and CSU/TOI trade at four-digit and three-digit prices. Use growth rates (per-share CAGR in the company's own reporting currency), margins, yields and coverage ratios, which are unit-free.
  2. Compute every ratio within one currency: numerator and denominator both in reporting currency. Where price is needed (FCF yield, P/E), convert the TSX price in CAD to the reporting currency at the same date, or convert the fundamental to CAD, and record the FX rate and date as a source.
  3. Per-share growth for USD/EUR reporters embeds no FX effect in reporting currency but does for a CAD investor; state which basis is used.
  4. Use a uniformly defined cash metric computed from the cash flow statement (e.g. operating cash flow minus capex minus lease principal payments, minus payments to non-controlling interests where relevant) rather than each company's own "free cash flow"; FCFA2S deducts items (interest, lease payments, IRGA/TSS liability, non-controlling share) that WSP/TFI/Aecon free cash flow definitions may not.
  5. Treat leases consistently in leverage (either all including lease liabilities with post-IFRS 16 EBITDA, or all excluding with pre-IFRS 16 EBITDA).
  6. For earnings, either add back amortisation of acquired intangibles for all five or for none; company "adjusted EPS" definitions are not interchangeable. CSU and Topicus do not publish an adjusted EPS at all in the sources found, which is itself a reason to prefer cash-based per-share metrics for the software pair.
  7. Flag one-off-distorted periods (Asseco dividend/fair value, WSP pre-funded equity, Aecon preferred-equity charge) with a note rather than silently using them.
- Because all five are IFRS filers, IFRS-vs-US-GAAP adjustments only matter when benchmarking against US peers (e.g. Old Dominion, Jacobs, AECOM, Roper).

### Gaps
- No authoritative analyst primer (CFA Institute or broker) on normalising serial-acquirer metrics across currencies was retrieved; the normalisation list is reasoned from disclosures.
- Exact line-item definitions of FCFA2S, WSP free cash flow, TFI free cash flow and Aecon adjusted EBITDA were not pulled from the MD&As in this session; they should be read from each company's non-IFRS measures section before computing a common metric.
- Confirmation that Aecon and WSP report under IFRS was not separately sourced here (standard for Canadian domestic issuers; WSP references IFRS 16 in its deal disclosure).

## Q4. How do thematic and multi-factor index providers convert scores into selection and weights, and what is most defensible for five names?

### Takeaway
The dominant factor-index pattern is: winsorise raw variables, convert to cross-sectional z-scores against a large parent universe, average into a composite, map to a positive score, then either select the top N and weight by score times market-cap weight (MSCI, S&P) or tilt all weights by a 0-1 cumulative-normal score (FTSE Russell), with issuer/sector caps and floors. Thematic and conviction-style indexes more often use equal weight or capped float-cap weight with simple min/max bounds (Morningstar Wide Moat Focus, Solactive thematics). Cross-sectional z-scores depend on a mean and standard deviation estimated from hundreds of names; with five names those estimates are dominated by single observations, so rank- or threshold-based scoring with equal or tiered weights and explicit caps is the more defensible design.

### Cited Findings
- MSCI Quality: the quality score combines z-scores of three winsorised fundamentals (ROE, debt-to-equity, earnings variability); variables are winsorised so the means used for standardisation are less affected by extremes; z = (x - mean)/standard deviation computed over the parent index universe excluding missing values; signs are flipped for debt/equity and earnings variability; the composite is the average of the three z-scores — [MSCI Quality Indexes Methodology (2013/14)](https://www.msci.com/eqb/methodology/meth_docs/MSCI_Quality_Indexes_Meth_Aug14.pdf); [MSCI Quality Indexes Methodology, May 2025](https://www.msci.com/indexes/documents/methodology/2_MSCI_Quality_Indexes_Methodology_20250520.pdf)
- MSCI maps the composite z-score to a strictly positive Quality Score: 1 + Z if Z > 0, and 1/(1 - Z) if Z < 0; a fixed number of securities is selected by ranking on that score, and weights are parent market-cap weights multiplied by the Quality Score — [MSCI Quality Indexes Methodology (2013/14)](https://www.msci.com/eqb/methodology/meth_docs/MSCI_Quality_Indexes_Meth_Aug14.pdf)
- Other MSCI factor methodologies winsorise z-scores at +/-3 — [MSCI Diversified Multiple-Factor Indexes Methodology](https://www.msci.com/eqb/methodology/meth_docs/2_MSCI_Diversified_Multi-Factor_Indexes_Methodology_20220524.pdf)
- S&P Quality: quality score is the average of z-scores of ROE, balance-sheet accruals ratio and financial leverage; constituents are weighted by the product of market capitalisation and quality score, subject to constraints; a stock with negative EPS or book value is assigned the lowest ROE z-score in the universe and is ineligible for the top-quintile quality indices — [S&P Quality Indices Methodology](https://spglobal.com/spdji/zh/documents/methodologies/methodology-sp-quality-indices.pdf); [S&P Indexology blog](https://www.indexologyblog.com/2022/10/31/the-climb-spiva-south-africa-mid-year-2022-scorecard/)
- S&P Quality FCF Aristocrats: float market cap times FCF score, with a 5% maximum stock weight and 40% maximum sector weight — [S&P DJI talking points](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/education/talking-points-introducing-the-sp-quality-fcf-aristocrats-suite.pdf)
- FTSE Russell tilt approach: start from an underlying weight (usually market cap), multiply by a factor score that is a cumulative-normal mapping of the z-score onto 0-1, optionally raised to an exponent to set tilt strength, then rescale to 100%; multi-factor indexes apply tilts multiplicatively in sequence rather than averaging factors; final weights are constrained with a maximum capacity ratio and a minimum stock weight — [FTSE Russell, Multi-factor indexes: the power of tilting](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/ftse-russell/en_us/documents/research/multi-factor-indexes-power-of-tilting.pdf); [FTSE Russell focused factor overview](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/ftse-russell/en_us/documents/other/focused-factor-overview.pdf)
- Morningstar Wide Moat Focus: eligibility screen (wide moat rating, fair value estimate not under review), rank by price/fair value, select a fixed count of the cheapest, equal-weight, with turnover buffers favouring existing members and a sector cap of the greater of 40% or benchmark weight plus 10%; staggered rebalancing — [Morningstar Wide Moat Focus construction rules](https://indexes.morningstar.com/api/docs/65d830dbafcda3cfd78a12bc); [Morningstar fact sheet](https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/bltabf2a7413d5a8f05/blt84a17c5f53bbc0df/5e6b996b9cca4006557d4784/Indexes-Wide-Moat-Focus-Fact-Sheet.pdf)
- Solactive thematic examples: float-market-cap weighting with a 4.0% maximum and 0.3% minimum company weight, excess redistributed proportionally (E-commerce index); equal weight across thematic categories (Smart City index); universe selection via sub-sector classification, third-party data or NLP — [Solactive SOLEBIZ guideline](https://solactive.com/downloads/Guideline-Solactive-SOLEBIZ.pdf); [Solactive SOLSC guideline](https://solactive.com/downloads/Guideline-Solactive-SOLSC.pdf); [Trackinsight interview](https://www.trackinsight.com/en/etf-news/indices-magic-beans-behind-successful-etfs)
- Liquidity screens in thematic indexes are typically a minimum market cap plus a minimum three-month average daily traded value (examples: at least US$800 thousand ADTV in a Nasdaq thematic index) — [Nasdaq CLMTCH methodology](https://indexes.nasdaqomx.com/docs/methodology_CLMTCH.pdf)
- Composite-indicator guidance lists several normalisation options (min-max, distance to reference, z-score, categorical scale, ranking, quantiles), notes that z-score and min-max are both sensitive to outliers, offers winsorisation as a remedy, and recommends testing alternative normalisations in a sensitivity analysis — [OECD/JRC Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/handbook-on-constructing-composite-indicators-methodology-and-user-guide_9789264043466-en.html); [JRC COIN normalisation training](https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/coin_2018_step_4_normalization.pdf)

### Inferences
- Why cross-sectional z-scores are unreliable at n = 5 (statistical reasoning, not from a cited source):
  - The mean and standard deviation are each estimated from five points, so one unusual company moves both; the "outlier" partly defines the yardstick it is measured against.
  - Z-scores are mathematically bounded by sample size: with the sample standard deviation the largest possible |z| is (n-1)/sqrt(n), which is about 1.79 for n = 5 (2.0 with the population formula). Winsorising at +/-3, as index providers do, can never bind, and percentile winsorisation (e.g. 5th/95th) is undefined for five points.
  - Adding or removing one candidate changes every other candidate's score, so the ranking is unstable to the composition of the candidate set.
  - The five names are in four sectors with structurally different margin, leverage and growth levels, so a cross-sectional z-score mostly measures sector, not quality. Index providers z-score against a parent universe of hundreds of stocks, often sector-relative.
- Options that remain defensible at n = 5, each with a provider precedent:
  1. Absolute threshold scoring: score each metric against pre-declared, sector-appropriate thresholds (pass/partial/fail or 0-5 points), sum to a composite. Stable to candidate-set changes and auditable. Closest precedent: eligibility screens (Morningstar moat/fair value; S&P negative-EPS rule).
  2. Rank-based scoring: rank 1-5 per metric and average ranks (Borda-style). Uses no spread estimate; loses magnitude information; listed as a normalisation option by OECD/JRC.
  3. Z-score against an external reference set (e.g. each company vs its sector peer group or the existing index constituents) rather than against the other four candidates. This is what MSCI/S&P actually do, but needs peer data.
- Converting scores to weights for a 15-18% sleeve:
  - Equal weight (Morningstar Wide Moat precedent): 3.0-3.6% each for five names. Most robust to scoring error; ignores conviction.
  - Score-tilted (MSCI/S&P/FTSE precedent): weight proportional to composite score (optionally times market cap), then apply a cap and floor and redistribute the excess proportionally (Solactive precedent). With five names, small score differences translate into large weight differences, so caps/floors do most of the work.
  - Tiered: bucket names into two or three conviction tiers with fixed weights per tier. A coarse version of score-tilting that does not over-read small score gaps.
  - Pure market-cap weighting would be dominated by CSU and WSP and is the least related to the scorecard.
- A selection step can precede weighting: a metric-based eligibility screen ("is this actually a serial acquirer?") may leave fewer than five names, mirroring how thematic indexes screen for theme purity before weighting.
- Common guardrails worth mirroring: single-name cap and floor, a liquidity/size screen, a sector cap (two of the five are VMS and Topicus is controlled by CSU, so they are economically related), a stated rebalance frequency, and a rule for missing data (MSCI excludes missing values from the mean/SD; S&P assigns the worst score).

### Gaps
- MSCI Quality: the exact winsorisation percentiles, the fixed-number/coverage selection rule, issuer cap and buffer rules in the May 2025 version could not be read (PDF not extractable here). The piecewise score mapping and weighting rule are from the 2013/14 version; assumed but not confirmed unchanged.
- S&P Quality Indices: winsorisation rule and exact weight caps were not retrieved.
- No MSCI thematic-index (relevance-score) methodology was retrieved.
- No Solactive guideline with explicit minimum market cap and ADTV thresholds was retrieved; the liquidity example is from Nasdaq.
- No published source was found that specifically discusses z-score reliability at very small n; that section is first-principles reasoning.

## Q5. Do existing "serial acquirer" or "compounder" funds or indexes publish selection criteria?

### Takeaway
No rules-based "serial acquirer" index with a published methodology was found. The closest published frameworks are qualitative: REQ Capital (manager of REQ Global Compounders) describes three pillars (capital allocation, decentralisation, people) and a set of red flags, and a third-party scorecard (Aktagon) publishes a quantitative ROIC-with-goodwill screen across 187 serial acquirers.

### Cited Findings
- REQ describes its fund as a concentrated portfolio of the best serial acquirers globally ("acquisition-driven compounders"), emphasising high free cash flow and capital allocation skill, and says it avoids turnarounds and companies with balance-sheet risk — [REQ full-year investment report 2023](https://req.no/2024/01/full-year-investment-report-2023); [REQ investor letter 2021](https://issuu.com/reqcapital/docs/investor_letter_2021/s/14445239)
- REQ published a 300+ page study on serial acquirers in December 2023 built around capital allocation, decentralisation and people; a condensed summary highlights five insights — [RollUpEurope summary of REQ primer](https://rollupeurope.beehiiv.com/p/utm-source-rss-utm-medium-rss-utm-campaign-reqs-serial-acquirers-primer-300-pages-condensed-into-5-i); [Quartr, three cornerstones of serial acquirer success](https://quartr.com/insights/company-research/the-three-cornerstones-of-serial-acquirer-success)
- Secondary summaries of REQ's work say successful serial acquirers are mostly founder-led or family-owned with long-tenured management, combine a high reinvestment rate with high return on capital ("dual engines of growth"), and list red flags: a change in M&A strategy, a change in financing, a cultural change, a change in management communication (fifth item truncated in source) — [The Investor's Podcast, TIP713 with Niklas Sävås](https://www.theinvestorspodcast.com/episodes/why-serial-acquirers-outperform-w-niklas-savas/); [Expanse Stocks, serial acquirers primer](https://expansestocks.substack.com/p/the-serial-acquirers-playbook-a-primer)
- Aktagon's serial acquirer scorecard screened 187 serial acquirers in 14 markets; only 59 earn above a 12% cost of equity with goodwill in the denominator; it decomposes ROIC into NOPAT margin and invested-capital turnover following Mauboussin and Callahan (2023); country pages report e.g. 6 of 16 US and 2 of 11 UK serial acquirers earning above cost of capital — [Aktagon scorecard](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/); [Aktagon ROIC analysis](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/roic-analysis/); [Aktagon US page](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/united-states/)
- A critique in the same literature: the preference for decentralisation in case-study-based work may reflect selection bias — [Outsider's Corner, Serial acquirers: the generalists](https://outsiderscorner.substack.com/p/serial-acquirers-the-generalists)

### Inferences
- REQ-style criteria translate into scorecard variables as: (a) returns on capital including goodwill above cost of capital, (b) reinvestment rate (share of FCF redeployed into M&A), (c) organic growth as the second engine, (d) low balance-sheet risk, (e) insider/founder ownership and management tenure, (f) decentralised structure, (g) consistency of M&A strategy and financing (no sudden shift to much larger deals or equity-funded deals).
- Applying the REQ red flags to the cited facts: CSU's 2019/2021 move to larger deals at lower hurdle rates and its 2025 founder transition, Topicus's Asseco minority stake, WSP's step-up to a US$3.3B deal partly equity-funded, and TFI's stated appetite for "something of size" are each a "change in pattern" that such a framework would require examining. This is a mapping of published criteria to published facts, not a judgement on the outcome.
- Aecon would fail the "avoid turnarounds" style criterion as REQ words it, given 2024-25 legacy losses, independent of whether it counts as a serial acquirer.

### Gaps
- REQ's own primary text for the five insights and its full red-flag list was not retrieved (only secondary summaries); REQ Global Compounders' current holdings and whether it holds any of the five were not checked.
- No broker "serial acquirer basket" methodology (e.g. from Nordic or Canadian brokers) was found in this session.
- No evidence was found of any index provider (MSCI, S&P, Solactive, FTSE Russell, Morningstar) publishing a dedicated serial-acquirer index methodology; this is a null search result, not proof that none exists.
