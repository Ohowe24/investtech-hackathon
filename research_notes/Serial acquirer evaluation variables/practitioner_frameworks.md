## 1. What metrics do dedicated serial-acquirer investors and research houses emphasise?

### Takeaway
Across REQ, Scott Management (via summaries), Redeye, Mauboussin, Thorndike and Mayer the hierarchy is consistent: (1) return on invested capital including goodwill, and its trend; (2) the ability to reinvest most free cash flow in small deals at those returns; (3) organic growth as the check that acquired businesses are healthy; (4) per-share outcomes (FCF/share), which net out dilution and leverage. Decentralisation and incentives are treated as the causal explanation, but are qualitative.

### Cited Findings

**REQ Capital (opinion, practitioner)**
- REQ published a 326-page serial-acquirer primer (Dec 2023); its summary attributes outperformance to three things: capital allocation, decentralisation, and people. [S] — [RollUpEurope summary of REQ primer](https://rollupeurope.com/p/utm-source-rss-utm-medium-rss-utm-campaign-reqs-serial-acquirers-primer-300-pages-condensed-into-5-i)
- Traits of good programmatic acquirers per the same summary: a rigorous target pipeline, internalised M&A expertise, a value-creation plan that survives closing, and a focus on small, private deals. [S] — [RollUpEurope](https://rollupeurope.com/p/utm-source-rss-utm-medium-rss-utm-campaign-reqs-serial-acquirers-primer-300-pages-condensed-into-5-i)
- Organic growth is described as a "litmus test" for individual acquisitions, looked at before group ROIC and EPS. [snip] — [RollUpEurope](https://rollupeurope.com/p/utm-source-rss-utm-medium-rss-utm-campaign-reqs-serial-acquirers-primer-300-pages-condensed-into-5-i)
- REQ's letters say its holdings have traditionally shown mid-single-digit organic growth and solid cash flows, with "dual growth engines" (organic plus acquisitions). [snip] — [REQ, Thriving in tough times](https://req.no/2023/12/thriving-in-tough-times)
- Example internal KPI REQ highlights: Momentum Group's target of P/WC > 45% (profit over working capital), the Bergman & Beving-family rule. [S] — [RollUpEurope](https://rollupeurope.com/p/utm-source-rss-utm-medium-rss-utm-campaign-reqs-serial-acquirers-primer-300-pages-condensed-into-5-i)

**Scott Management "Serial Acquirers" paper (opinion; read only via a summary)**
- The paper (originally by Ryan Krafft) is called the seminal piece; it classifies acquirers as Hold-cos (unrelated, no synergies, e.g. Berkshire), Roll-ups (one homogeneous vertical, scale synergies), Platforms (a few focused platforms, skills transfer, e.g. Danaher), and Accumulators (many verticals, no synergy-chasing, e.g. Constellation). Platforms and accumulators "hold the most promise for long-term capital compounding." [S] — [Exploring Context, Studying Serial Acquirers](https://exploringcontext.substack.com/p/studying-serial-acquirers)
- The follow-on framework ranks companies on two axes: (1) cumulative M&A spend over 5 years (capacity to deploy) and (2) ROIC + 1/2 organic revenue growth (quality of deployment). The half-weight on growth is the author's admittedly "arbitrary" correction for ROIC drifting upward as old deals age in book value. [S] — [Exploring Context](https://exploringcontext.substack.com/p/studying-serial-acquirers)
- "Law of diminishing returns to M&A": as deal size and cash to deploy grow, incremental returns fall; platforms/accumulators "rarely stay materially above 10-12 deals a year"; deals of $500M-$1B at ~20x EV/EBITA make double-digit long-run ROIC hard; roll-up returns fade once ~20-30% of realistic TAM is consolidated. [S] — [Exploring Context](https://exploringcontext.substack.com/p/studying-serial-acquirers)

**Redeye (Swedish serial acquirers; opinion plus sector data)**
- Redeye runs a serial-acquirers index and quarterly sector updates; the Q2 2024 update is devoted to comparing ROIC and ROE "on an apples-to-apples basis" across its index, i.e. it treats ROIC comparability as the central analytical problem. [P, abstract only] — [Redeye press release via MFN](https://storage.mfn.se/fcf93186-987d-452e-9cf8-1027c05374e8/serial-acquirers-q2-2024-comparing-roic-on-an-apples-to-apples-basis.pdf); [Redeye research page](https://redeye.se/research/1037687/serial-acquirers-q2-2024-comparing-roic-on-an-apples-to-apples-basis)
- Redeye is quoted as saying multiples paid have been roughly constant for 20 years, "mostly ranging 5-8X [of EBITA] based on initial purchase consideration." [snip] — [RollUpEurope, Redeye 2025 conference notes](https://rollupeurope.beehiiv.com/p/redeye-2025-serial-acquirers-conference-day-1-notes)

**Mauboussin / Morgan Stanley Counterpoint Global (data-backed, all US equities, not serial-acquirer specific)**
- "ROIC and the Investment Process" (2023): rising ROIC goes with stronger TSR and falling ROIC with weaker TSR; moves between ROIC quintiles are where outsized returns occur. Sample: US public companies 1990-2022. [snip] — [Morgan Stanley article page](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-au/institutional-investor/insights/articles/roic-and-the-investment-process.html); [PDF](https://eatonvance.com/content/dam/im/assets/publication/thought-leadership/consilient-observer/article_roicandtheinvestmentprocess.pdf)
- Figures attributed to Mauboussin & Callahan (2023) by a third party (not verified against the PDF): ROIC correlates r=0.58 with value creation, r=0.78 when combined with growth; bottom-to-top ROIC quintile movers earned 33% annualised TSR over three years, top-to-bottom movers -11%; 48% of top-quintile firms remain top-quintile after three years, 15% fall to the bottom. [S, unverified] — [Aktagon scorecard methodology](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/how-to-analyze/)
- Mauboussin notes ROIIC, ROIC and IRR are "often used without full acknowledgment of their limitations", and that judging the payoff from investments is tricky "even for relatively large and discrete deals such as M&A." [snip] — [Market-Expected Return on Investment](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/publication/insights/articles/article_marketexpectedreturnoninvestment_en.pdf); [Consilient Observer series](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-gr/institutional-investor/insights/series/consilient-observer.html)

**Thorndike, "The Outsiders" (opinion; case-study evidence, 8 CEOs)**
- Core tenets as summarised: capital allocation is the CEO's most important job; "what counts in the long run is per share value, not overall growth or size"; cash flow, not reported earnings, determines value; decentralised organisations; buybacks when the stock is cheap. CEOs have five uses of capital: reinvest, acquire, pay dividends, pay down debt, repurchase shares. [S] — [Stanford GSB book page](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/alumni/news/books/outsiders-eight-unconventional-ceos-their-radically-rational-blueprint); [7 Circles summary of Mayer/Thorndike](https://the7circles.uk/?p=35655)
- Teledyne illustration used by REQ: 130 acquisitions in the 1960s-70s, then repurchased ~85% of its shares; a 1966 investor earned 17.9% p.a. (53x) over 25 years vs 6.7x for the S&P 500. [S] — [RollUpEurope](https://rollupeurope.com/p/utm-source-rss-utm-medium-rss-utm-campaign-reqs-serial-acquirers-primer-300-pages-condensed-into-5-i)

**Chris Mayer, "100 Baggers" (opinion; descriptive study of past 100-baggers)**
- Central idea: own businesses with high returns on capital that can reinvest at those high rates for a long time; owner-operators with skin in the game; draws on Thorndike's CEOs. [S] — [book review](https://oldmandan.squarespace.com/essays/100-baggers-by-chris-mayer); [7 Circles](https://the7circles.uk/?p=35655)

**Aktagon Global Serial Acquirer Scorecard (independent screen; cross-sectional data, no backtest)**
- 187 listed serial acquirers in 14 markets (definition: 3+ acquisitions in a decade, M&A a stated strategy). Six tests: ROIC with goodwill > 12%; goodwill/total assets; operating margin relative to goodwill load; drawdown; 3-year ROIC trend; standing vs own-market pass rate. 59 of 187 pass the 12% ROIC test, 128 fail. [P for the screen's own claims] — [Aktagon methodology](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/how-to-analyze/)
- It claims acquisition count/pace, revenue growth, past management track record and headline margins do NOT separate winners; ROIC with goodwill, goodwill/TA trend, margin vs goodwill burden and ROIC direction do. Above 40% goodwill/TA, only 24% earn >12% ROIC (vs 35% below). Author is unnamed; evidence is same-period cross-section, not predictive. [P] — [Aktagon methodology](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/how-to-analyze/)

### Inferences
- The summed metric "ROIC + organic growth" (Constellation) and its damped variant "ROIC + 1/2 organic growth" (Exploring Context) are the closest thing to an agreed single score among practitioners.
- Every framework insists goodwill stays in the capital base when judging the acquirer (as opposed to judging the underlying businesses). A scoring tool should use goodwill-inclusive ROIC as the primary variable and may show ex-goodwill return as a business-quality side note.
- Investors value "capacity to redeploy" (reinvestment rate) alongside return; high ROIC with nothing to buy is worth less.

### Gaps
- REQ primer, Scott Management paper and Redeye full report were not read in original; exact metric definitions and any explicit ranking of metrics in those documents are unconfirmed.
- No verified Credit Suisse HOLT / Mauboussin "Capital Allocation" M&A-specific statistics were retrieved (PDFs blocked).
- Book claims (Outsiders, 100 Baggers) are via reviews, not page-cited.

## 2. How are the individual metrics defined and used, and which are computable from standard statements?

### Takeaway
Most of the core set (goodwill-inclusive ROIC, ROIC trend, reinvestment rate, FCF/share growth, share-count change, net debt/EBITDA, cash conversion, goodwill/assets) is computable from standard statements; organic growth, multiples paid, deal count/size, decentralisation and incentive design depend on voluntary disclosure.

### Cited Findings

- **ROIC, Constellation definition**: Adjusted Net Income / Average Invested Capital, where ANI adds back non-cash amortisation of intangibles and Invested Capital is a running tally of shareholders' money (start estimate + ANI - dividends + share issuance). It is therefore an equity (levered) return. [P] — [CSI letters, merged PDF](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf). An outside analyst notes the same: Leonard's "ROIC" is levered, and what others call ROIC Leonard calls "EBITA returns". [S] — [Exploring Context](https://exploringcontext.substack.com/p/studying-serial-acquirers)
- **ROIC, screening definition**: NOPAT (operating income x (1 - tax rate)) / (equity + debt - cash), goodwill included; 12% used as hurdle. [P] — [Aktagon](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/how-to-analyze/)
- **ROTIC (Halma, Judges Scientific)**: post-tax adjusted profit / average total invested capital, with goodwill and acquired intangibles kept in at original cost (including amounts previously written off). Halma has reported it since at least 2008 (14.1% then, against a much higher ROCE). [snip] — [Halma FY results](https://www.halma.com/news/press-releases/2024/full-year-results-2023-24); [ShareScope commentary](https://knowledge.sharescope.co.uk/2024/07/03/elephants-can-gallop/)
- **Return on tangible / operating capital (Lifco)**: EBITA / average capital employed excluding goodwill; 132% at end-2025 and 127% at 30 Sep 2026. Measures quality of the underlying businesses, not price paid. [snip] — [Lifco interim report Jan-Sep 2026](https://mfn.se/a/lifco/interim-report-january-september-2026)
- **CRI (Roper)**: (net income + D&A - maintenance capex) / (net working capital + net PP&E + accumulated depreciation); management prefers it because acquirers show "artificially low" ROIC. Note it excludes goodwill, so it also measures business quality, not deal price. [snip] — [Commoncog Roper case](https://commoncog.com/c/cases/roper-manufacturing-software/); [Scuttleblurb](https://scuttleblurb.substack.com/p/rop-roper-technologies)
- **Incremental ROIC (ROIIC)**: Leonard's formula (ANI1 - ANI0) / (IC1 - IC0); he likes it conceptually but calls it "very volatile", and says share issuance or buybacks "can throw it out of kilter". [P] — [CSI letter dated April 2018](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf)
- **Reinvestment rate**: not formally defined in the sources read; described as reinvesting "all or most of their free cash flow for a very long time at high rates of return", with deals "funded primarily by cash flows". [S] — [Exploring Context](https://exploringcontext.substack.com/p/studying-serial-acquirers); [snip] [Arbor, serial acquirer business model](https://arborpermanentowners.beehiiv.com/p/serial-acquirer-business-model)
- **FCF per share growth**: (FCF1/shares1) / (FCF0/shares0) - 1; captures share count, interest and capex. [P] — [CSI letter dated April 2018](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf)
- **Organic growth**: growth excluding acquisitions (and usually FX). Halma targets >5% organic revenue growth (in place since at least FY2007); Judges reports 19-year revenue CAGR 19% vs organic 7%. [snip] — [Halma 2007 report](https://files.investis.com/halma-reports/AR2007/overview.html); [Judges FY25 results](https://www.ticker.app/lse/JDG/rns/2026-03-31/Unaudited-Preliminary-Results/7124Y)
- **Cash conversion**: Halma: adjusted operating cash flow / adjusted operating profit, target 90%. Danaher: FCF / net income (about 145% in 2025; 34th straight year above 100%). Judges: 118% in FY25. [snip] — [Halma FY2024/25 results](https://halma.com/news/press-releases/2025/full-year-results-2024-25); [Danaher Q4 call coverage](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/danaher-q4-earnings-call-highlights-150957156.html); [Judges FY25](https://www.ticker.app/lse/JDG/rns/2026-03-31/Unaudited-Preliminary-Results/7124Y)
- **Acquisition multiples paid (EV/EBITA)**: Swedish serial acquirers mostly pay 5-8x EBITA on initial consideration (Redeye, quoted); first-year return on M&A is estimated around 10-11% on a cash basis. [snip] — [RollUpEurope](https://rollupeurope.beehiiv.com/p/redeye-2025-serial-acquirers-conference-day-1-notes); [Compounding Tortoise](https://thecompoundingtortoise.substack.com/p/a-closer-look-at-our-serial-acquirers)
- **Share count / dilution**: "it is no achievement to grow revenues or cash flow 50% per annum while growing share count by 100% per annum" (reason Leonard reports per-share metrics); he links intrinsic value per share to an "unchanging share count". [P] — [CSI letters (2009 and 2011/2012 letters)](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf)
- **Leverage**: Lifco caps interest-bearing net debt at 3x EBITDA (1.2x at 30 Jun 2026). [snip] — [Lifco interim report Jan-Jun 2026](https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/lifco-interim-report-january-june-2026-20260714). Leonard: leverage "can have a profound impact on ROIC"; avoid short-term debt funding long-term assets. [P] — [CSI 2015 letter](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf)
- **Goodwill / total assets**: bands <15% organic, 15-30% disciplined, 30-40% moderate, >40% heavy. [P] — [Aktagon](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/how-to-analyze/)
- **Decentralisation and incentives**: cited as a cause of outperformance by REQ; incentive pay tied to M&A volume or revenue is a red flag; Constellation's bonus formula is driven by ROIC and growth. [S]/[P] — [RollUpEurope](https://rollupeurope.com/p/utm-source-rss-utm-medium-rss-utm-campaign-reqs-serial-acquirers-primer-300-pages-condensed-into-5-i); [CSI 2015 letter](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf)

### Inferences

Computability (my classification, based on the definitions above):

| Metric | Computable from standard statements? | Inputs / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| ROIC incl. goodwill (NOPAT / (equity + debt - cash)) | Yes | Operating income, tax, equity, debt, cash. If only EPS/net income is available, an equity-return variant (Leonard-style) is possible but is flattered by leverage |
| ROIC trend (3-5y) | Yes | Same inputs over time |
| ROIIC | Yes, but noisy | Change in profit / change in invested capital; use multi-year windows |
| Reinvestment rate | Yes | Acquisition spend (cash flow statement) / operating cash flow or FCF |
| Cumulative M&A spend | Yes | Acquisition spend line |
| FCF per share growth | Yes | FCF, share count |
| Share count change | Yes | Shares outstanding (or net income / EPS) |
| Net debt / EBITDA | Yes | Debt, cash, EBITDA (leases and earn-outs often omitted) |
| Cash conversion (OCF or FCF / net income or EBITA) | Yes | Definition varies by company |
| Goodwill / total assets | Yes if balance sheet detail available | Not in the minimal list (revenue, EPS, OCF, FCF, debt, cash, acquisition spend) |
| ROIC + organic growth | Partly | Needs organic growth |
| Organic growth | No (disclosure) | Rough proxy only: total growth minus acquired growth implied by acquisition spend; unreliable |
| Multiples paid (EV/EBITA) | No (disclosure) | Rarely disclosed deal by deal |
| Deal count / average deal size | Partly | Count needs disclosure; average size = spend / count |
| Return on tangible capital / ROTIC / CRI | Partly | Needs goodwill, intangibles, working capital, sometimes cumulative write-offs |
| Decentralisation, insider ownership, incentive design | No | Proxy filings / annual report text; qualitative |

- Because company-reported KPIs use incompatible definitions (levered ANI/IC, ROTIC, ex-goodwill ROCE, CRI), a ranking tool must compute one consistent definition itself rather than ingest reported figures.

### Gaps
- No source read gave a formal numeric definition of "reinvestment rate" or a threshold for insider ownership.
- Redeye's apples-to-apples ROIC adjustments (the actual method) were not obtained.

## 3. What red flags do they cite for roll-ups that fail?

### Takeaway
The recurring warning signs are weak or undisclosed organic growth, reliance on adjusted earnings, leverage (and its concealment), equity-funded and ever-larger deals, incentives tied to deal volume, and falling goodwill-inclusive ROIC. Historic base rates for roll-ups are poor.

### Cited Findings
- REQ-derived red-flag list: company stops disclosing organic growth or keeps redefining it; leverage concealed by excluding debt items or creative net debt/EBITDA; large insider selling alongside capital raises; several acquisitions repeatedly press-released at once; CEO departs soon after a very large deal; incentive pay tied to M&A volume or revenue; little transparency on prices paid and earn-out terms. (Opinion.) [S] — [RollUpEurope](https://rollupeurope.com/p/utm-source-rss-utm-medium-rss-utm-campaign-reqs-serial-acquirers-primer-300-pages-condensed-into-5-i)
- Valeant, adjusted earnings: Barron's/New Constructs called reliance on non-GAAP metrics a red flag; Valeant reported a GAAP loss of $292 million in 2015 despite positive adjusted figures. [snip] — [New Constructs / Barron's](https://www.newconstructs.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Valeant-Doesn’t-Look-Cheap-Barrons_NewConstructs2015-05-07.pdf)
- Valeant, organic growth: in 2014 analysts flagged weak or negative organic growth as "the biggest potential issue"; a Veritas estimate put organic revenue at -2.5% in Q1 once discontinued products were included. [snip] — [Motley Fool, 2014](https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2014/05/17/1-valeant-concern-investors-should-watch.aspx); [Globe and Mail, 2014](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/amp/globe-investor/investment-ideas/valeant-needs-a-big-dose-of-allergan/article18669133/)
- Roll-up base rates: a Booz Allen study of 81 roll-ups (1993-2000) found only 11 beat the market; another study found more than two-thirds created no value. (Data-backed, cited second-hand in search results; original not located.) [snip] — [Corner of Berkshire & Fairfax thread](https://thecobf.com/forum/topic/10199-specialty-drug-industry-the-alchemy-of-finance)
- Dye & Durham: by late 2025 it had failed to complete its FY2025 audit and was under a management cease-trade order; one data provider shows debt of CA$1.65bn vs equity CA$305m and interest coverage of 0.9x. [snip] — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/financial-accounting/dye-durham-shares-sink-as-filing-delay-threatens-turnaround); [Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stocks/ca/software/tsx-dnd/dye-durham-shares/health)
- Rising deal size / falling hurdle rates: Leonard found "hurdle rates are magnetic": when CSI lowered its hurdle rate, expected IRRs on all subsequent deals fell to the new level, not just marginal ones. His stated main concern for discipline was being "tempted to drop our hurdle rates as our cash balances climb." (Internal data, 1995-2015 vintages.) [P] — [CSI 2015 letter and letter dated April 2017](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf)
- Large deals: McKinsey reports that buying a target worth 30%+ of the acquirer's market cap has "just an even chance of success", vs 65% of programmatic acquirers outperforming. [S] — [CFO Dive on McKinsey](https://www.cfodive.com/news/programmatic-dealmaking-strategy-pays-off-most-mckinsey/608412/)
- Heavy goodwill with thin margins: above 40% goodwill/total assets, 76% of screened acquirers earn below a 12% ROIC; 3+ consecutive years of declining ROIC is flagged. [P] — [Aktagon](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/how-to-analyze/)
- Leverage flattering returns: some conglomerates raised ROIC by borrowing to shrink invested capital; one "returned their entire Invested Capital to shareholders, and hence generates an infinite ROIC". [P] — [CSI 2015 letter](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf)
- Amortisation add-backs: Leonard concedes managers "tend to ignore these 'expenses', focusing on EBITA or EBITDA or 'Adjusted' Net Income"; ignoring amortisation implicitly assumes the acquired asset's life is perpetual, which is only sometimes true. [P] — [CSI 2007 Q4 letter](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf)

### Inferences
- Quantifiable red-flag variables from standard statements: net debt/EBITDA level and trend; share-count growth; gap between FCF and reported/adjusted earnings (cash conversion); declining goodwill-inclusive ROIC; acquisition spend exceeding operating cash flow for several years (deals funded externally).
- Negative organic growth is the single most-cited failure signal but is disclosure-dependent; its absence from disclosure is itself treated as a flag.

### Gaps
- No primary short report or forensic write-up was retrieved for Dye & Durham or Carillion; nothing sourced here on restructuring charges, goodwill impairments or acquisition-accounting abuse specifically. These remain commonly asserted but unsourced in these notes.
- The Booz Allen roll-up study was not located in original.

## 4. What do Constellation (Mark Leonard) and other top acquirers use as their own KPIs?

### Takeaway
Leonard's letters trace a clear evolution: ROIC + organic net revenue growth as the single best metric (2007-2015), doubts from 2015, then growth in FCF per share as the best proxy for intrinsic value growth (April 2018 letter), with IRR used internally for deals. Other elite acquirers publish return-on-total-capital, organic growth and cash conversion targets.

### Cited Findings

**Constellation Software (all [P], from the merged letters PDF: [link](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf))**
- 2007 Q4 letter: "Our favourite single metric for measuring our corporate performance is the sum of ROIC and Organic Net Revenue Growth ('ROIC+OGr')." The glossary says the metric "provides a historical measure of the effectiveness of our capital allocation."
- Reported levels: 43% (2011), 37% (2012), 39% (2013), 40% (2014), 35% (2015), 33% (2017). In 2011-2012 letters it is called the best single metric for "short-term performance of our low asset intensity software businesses."
- Per-share logic (letter of March 2010): Leonard uses Revenue per Share and Cash Flow from Operating Activities per Share as long-run shareholder-value measures, and checks that growth "has not been achieved at the cost of significantly increased debt per share."
- 2015 letter, limits of the metric: in asset-light businesses the historical invested capital "becomes increasingly irrelevant over time" (some units' EBITA exceeds their purchase price, pushing ROIC toward infinity). ROIC+OGr is "a reasonable (but somewhat overstated) proxy" only if all FCF is reinvested at historical economics; with higher multiples paid or significant debt, it "can quickly become misleading."
- 2015 letter, internal measure: "When we judge our own track record, we use IRR", updated quarterly for each acquisition (245 acquisitions since 2004); not disclosed for competitive reasons. IRR's faults: reinvestment assumptions and it shows neither hold period nor size.
- 2015 letter, hurdle rates: lower hurdle for larger transactions, original hurdles for most; this "will likely drive down our future ROIC." "Hurdle rates are magnetic."
- 2015 letter, peer comparison: CSI studies "high performance conglomerates" (HPCs) with a decade-plus of superior returns. Most HPCs ran ROIC in the mid-to-high teens over the decade; CSI averaged 30%. HPCs "struggled to increase their ANI per share by more than 15% per annum"; only a couple using significant leverage exceeded that. CSI's own ANI/share grew 37% p.a. over the decade.
- 2015 letter, incentives: ROIC is "one of the big drivers of our incentive compensation program", growth is "the other factor in the bonus formula"; very-high-ROIC groups are told to "keep your capital" and redeploy it.
- Letter dated April 2018 (FY2017): "Over time, ROIC+OGr should move asymptotically towards our hurdle rate if we are deploying all of our FCF on acquisitions." Seeking a new single best metric, he rejects ROIIC as too volatile and Net Maintenance Revenue per share as not derivable from audited statements, and concludes: "If I had to stake my reputation on one of those as the best proxy for the annual increase in intrinsic value, I would go with the increase in FCF per share." CFO Jamal Baksh prefers ANI per share (less volatile). Three-year averages given: ROIC+OGr 33%, market cap +28% p.a., FCF/share +16% p.a., ANI/share +20% p.a. He adds: "you don't need to use any single metric."
- FCFA2S (free cash flow available to shareholders) appears in later letters; the 2021 letter says FCFA2S would go mainly to small and mid-sized VMS acquisitions at traditional hurdle rates, with a lower hurdle for large deals. [snip, not in the merged PDF] — [Hedge Fund Alpha on the Feb 2021 letter](https://hedgefundalpha.com/investor-letters/mark-leonard-its-time-to-lower-hurdle-rates/)
- Why not EPS/EBITDA: the letters do not contain a single anti-EPS statement in the passages read. ## 5. Is there quantitative backtest evidence that these metrics predicted returns among serial acquirers?

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and mostly not serial-acquirer specific. The best-supported findings are McKinsey's programmatic-M&A result (many small deals beat large or no deals on excess TSR) and Mauboussin's all-market result that ROIC improvement tracks TSR. No rigorous, out-of-sample backtest of ROIC, organic growth or deal-size metrics within a serial-acquirer universe was found.

### Cited Findings
- McKinsey (data-backed, correlational): in a dataset of 1,000 global companies over 2007-2017, programmatic acquirers delivered about 2% p.a. more excess TSR than peers; no other approach (organic, selective, large-deal) produced positive excess TSR on average; programmatic had the lowest dispersion and the largest share with positive excess TSR (65%). A later Global 2,000 update reports 3.9% excess TSR in the most recent decade vs 2.9% in the 2010s. McKinsey cautions it is "not purely a volume play." [snip for figures; article timed out] — [McKinsey, Repeat performance](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/repeat-performance-the-continuing-case-for-programmatic-m-and-a); [McKinsey, How one approach to M&A...](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/how-one-approach-to-m-and-a-is-more-likely-to-create-value-than-all-others); corroborated [S] by [CFO Dive](https://www.cfodive.com/news/programmatic-dealmaking-strategy-pays-off-most-mckinsey/608412/)
- Mauboussin & Callahan (data-backed, all US stocks 1990-2022): TSR tracks changes in ROIC; detailed figures in section 1 are second-hand. [snip] — [Morgan Stanley](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-au/institutional-investor/insights/articles/roic-and-the-investment-process.html)
- Constellation's HPC study (company-internal, small sample): an equal-weighted portfolio of the high-performance conglomerates it studied "more than doubled the performance of the S&P 500" over the prior decade. Selection was on past success, so this is survivorship-biased by construction. [P] — [CSI 2015 letter](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf)
- Roll-up base rate (data-backed, old, second-hand): 11 of 81 roll-ups beat the market, 1993-2000. [snip] — [thread citing Booz Allen](https://thecobf.com/forum/topic/10199-specialty-drug-industry-the-alchemy-of-finance)
- Aktagon (cross-sectional, not a backtest): only 59 of 187 serial acquirers earn >12% goodwill-inclusive ROIC; in Sweden 6 of 24. The page states it has no portfolio return study. [P] — [Aktagon methodology](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/how-to-analyze/); [Sweden page](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/sweden/)
- Anecdotal long-run returns of the Bergman & Beving family (case evidence, not a test): Lagercrantz ~21% CAGR since 2001 spin; Addtech 21.5% CAGR 2001-2016; a 1999 B&B investment including spin-offs up ~38x. [S] — [Exploring Context](https://exploringcontext.substack.com/p/studying-serial-acquirers)
- An academic thesis on serial acquirers surfaced in search but was not read. [snip] — [University of Vaasa repository](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/800e1e38-76bf-4923-bcee-29a3bc191bd6/content)

### Inferences
- The index methodology can claim practitioner consensus and partial empirical support (programmatic small deals; ROIC level and direction), but should not claim that its scoring variables are backtested predictors of returns among serial acquirers.
- Survivorship bias pervades the genre: most frameworks are built from winners (CSI, Lifco, Danaher). The Aktagon cross-section (roughly two-thirds failing a 12% ROIC test) is the main counterweight found.

### Gaps
- No backtest found for: organic growth as a return predictor within serial acquirers; multiples paid vs subsequent returns; insider ownership vs returns in this universe; REQ's own performance statistics for its serial-acquirer universe (reportedly in the primer, not accessed).
- McKinsey's precise category definitions (deal count and cumulative deal value thresholds for "programmatic") were not retrieved.
