# Seven variables separate compounders from empire builders

The evidence points to a short scorecard built on returns and funding, not on deal count: **goodwill-inclusive ROIC (level and trend), organic growth, FCF-per-share growth, net share issuance, self-funded reinvestment rate, deal-size concentration and cadence, and leverage used as a guardrail rather than a score**. Academic M&A research is strongest on how deals are paid for and how big they are: stock-financed and outsized deals destroy value, while steady programmes of small private deals do not show the declining returns once attributed to serial acquirers. Practitioners, led by Constellation's own shareholder letters, supply the return metrics (ROIC plus organic growth, then FCF per share), but no rigorous out-of-sample backtest of these metrics inside a serial-acquirer universe was found, so the scorecard rests on consensus and indirect evidence, not proven predictive power. Six of the seven variables are computable from standard statements; organic growth, deal counts, multiples paid and decentralisation depend on company disclosure. Across sectors the variables hold but the inputs change: FCFA2S and FX-neutral organic growth for software, net-revenue organic growth and a leverage band for engineering, through-cycle operating ratio for trucking, and backlog quality and margin excluding legacy losses for construction. For five names in four sectors, cross-sectional z-scores are statistically meaningless; pre-declared thresholds, an eligibility gate, and equal or tiered weights with caps are the defensible design. On the facts gathered, Aecon is the candidate whose membership in the theme is weakest.

## Funding and deal size predict outcomes better than deal count

The most replicated result in the M&A literature concerns payment method and target type, not acquisitiveness itself. In a sample of 12,023 US acquisitions from 1980 to 2001, public-target deals averaged a **-1.02% announcement return and lost 5.9 cents per dollar spent**, while private and subsidiary acquisitions gained, and cash beat stock only for public targets ([NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/aug03/big-firms-lose-value-acquisitions)). Fuller, Netter and Stegemoller isolated the effect by holding the bidder constant across firms making five or more deals: returns were negative for public targets paid in stock and positive for private and subsidiary targets regardless of payment ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v57y2002i4p1763-1793.html)). The long-run record agrees: acquirers completing stock mergers earned **-25.0% excess returns over five years** ([Loughran and Vijh](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:52:y:1997:i:5:p:1765-90)), a penalty later work attributes largely to the externally financed asset growth that stock deals produce ([JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/postacquisition-returns-of-stock-deals-evidence-of-the-pervasiveness-of-the-asset-growth-effect/68D4383F00A213F47CFCCA83E0865C59)). Overpriced equity used as currency also predicts later goodwill write-offs ([Gu and Lev](https://archive.nyu.edu/handle/2451/27476)), and impairments in turn lead declines in profitability ([Review of Accounting Studies](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11142-011-9167-2)). The practical translation is that **net share issuance is the cleanest statement-based funding variable**, with goodwill impairment history as a lagging confirmation.

The second robust finding is about the tail. Between 1998 and 2001, "large loss deals" were **2.1% of acquisitions but lost $397bn while all other deals gained $157bn**, and they were made by richly valued firms that had previously been successful acquirers ([NBER w10200](https://www.nber.org/papers/w10200)). That is the empire-builder signature: a step-change in deal size after a good run. Leonard describes the same mechanism from the inside, noting that "hurdle rates are magnetic" and that his main worry was the temptation to drop them as cash balances climb ([Constellation letters](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf)).

Deal frequency, by contrast, is not a warning sign. Earlier studies found announcement returns declining across a CEO's deal sequence and read it as overconfidence ([Billett and Qian](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:54:y:2008:i:6:p:1037-1051)), but newer work finds the decline concentrated in bursts of acquisitions and absent for the most active acquirers ([Morillon](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/finrev/v56y2021i3p407-432.html)), and absent for the most frequent serial acquirers once market anticipation is controlled for ([Macias, Rau and Stouraitis](https://academic.oup.com/rcfs/article/14/1/35/7225178)). Three consulting datasets point the same way on long-run returns: McKinsey's programmatic acquirers earned a median **2.1% a year of excess TSR** while other approaches earned zero or less ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/m-and-a/our-insights/the-seven-habits-of-programmatic-acquirers)); Bain found frequent acquirers at 10.1% to 11.0% annual TSR against **5.1% for "selective large bets", the worst group** ([Bain](https://bain.com/insights/how-to-create-value-m-and-a-report-2022)); BCG's "portfolio builders" averaged 11.6% against 3.5% for one-timers and non-acquirers ([BCG](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2015/10/12/924250/0/en/Strategic-Acquirers-Create-More-Value-Than-Nonacquisitive-Companies.html?print=1)). These sorts classify firms over the same window in which returns are measured and lose the firms that stumbled, so they justify the theme more than they validate frequency as a scoring variable. The usable variable is **steadiness of cadence plus low single-deal concentration**, not raw deal count.

One further result frames the whole exercise. Acquirer fixed effects explain about as much of the variation in acquirer returns as all deal and firm characteristics combined, and the persistence sits at the top of the distribution and survives CEO changes ([Golubov, Yawson and Zhang](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:116:y:2015:i:2:p:314-330)). Skill is a firm trait, which argues for track-record variables such as realised returns on acquired capital over deal-characteristic variables. It also sits against a headwind every serial acquirer must overcome: asset growth negatively predicts returns for up to five years ([Cooper, Gulen and Schill](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:63:y:2008:i:4:p:1609-1651)). Growth quantity should never be scored positively; growth quality should.

## Practitioners converge on goodwill-inclusive ROIC, organic growth and per-share cash

Constellation's letters give the clearest account of which return metric to trust and when it fails. From 2007 Leonard's "favourite single metric" was the sum of ROIC and organic net revenue growth, reported at 43% in 2011 and 33% in 2017. By 2015 he was warning that in asset-light businesses historical invested capital "becomes increasingly irrelevant over time" and that the metric "can quickly become misleading" under higher multiples or significant debt. In the April 2018 letter he rejected incremental ROIC as "very volatile" and concluded that, staking his reputation on one proxy for intrinsic value growth, he "would go with the increase in FCF per share" ([Constellation letters](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf)). The lesson for a scorecard is to use **ROIC as the quality-of-deployment measure and FCF-per-share growth as the outcome measure**, and to treat incremental ROIC only as a multi-year cross-check.

Two definitional points matter more than the choice of metric. First, goodwill stays in the denominator. A third-party screen of 187 listed serial acquirers found that **only 59 earn more than 12% ROIC with goodwill included**, and that above 40% goodwill to total assets only 24% clear that bar; the same screen claims that deal count, revenue growth and headline margins do not separate winners, though it is a same-period cross-section with no return study ([Aktagon](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/how-to-analyze/)). Ex-goodwill measures such as Lifco's return on capital employed measure the quality of the businesses bought, not the price paid for them, and are useful only as a side note. Second, company-reported returns are not interchangeable: Constellation's "ROIC" is adjusted net income over a running tally of shareholders' capital, a levered equity return, and Leonard himself observed that some conglomerates raised ROIC by borrowing to shrink invested capital ([Constellation letters](https://www.mbi-deepdives.com/content/files/2022/07/Constellation-Software-SH-Letters-Merged.pdf)). The engine must compute one definition for all five companies instead of ingesting reported figures.

Organic growth is the most-cited health check and the most-cited failure signal. REQ Capital's work, as summarised, treats it as the litmus test for whether acquired businesses are sound, and lists a company ceasing to disclose it, incentive pay tied to deal volume, concealed leverage and a shift in financing or deal size among its red flags ([RollUpEurope](https://rollupeurope.com/p/utm-source-rss-utm-medium-rss-utm-campaign-reqs-serial-acquirers-primer-300-pages-condensed-into-5-i)). One practitioner framework ranks acquirers on two axes, five-year cumulative M&A spend and ROIC plus half of organic growth, with the half-weight an admittedly arbitrary correction for ROIC drifting up as old deals age ([Exploring Context](https://exploringcontext.substack.com/p/studying-serial-acquirers)). That two-axis shape, capacity to deploy and quality of deployment, is the right mental model: high ROIC with nothing to buy is worth less than slightly lower ROIC with a long runway. Decentralisation is offered as the causal explanation throughout this literature, but it is qualitative, and critics note the preference may reflect selection on winners ([Outsider's Corner](https://outsiderscorner.substack.com/p/serial-acquirers-the-generalists)). It belongs in the human-written reasons, not in the score.

The evidence base has real limits. The broad-market support for ROIC is Mauboussin's finding that TSR tracks changes in ROIC across US equities ([Morgan Stanley](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/en-au/institutional-investor/insights/articles/roic-and-the-investment-process.html)); nothing found tests ROIC, organic growth or multiples paid as predictors within serial acquirers specifically. Nearly all academic evidence is US data, with no Canadian large-sample study. The methodology should claim practitioner consensus and partial empirical support, not backtested predictors.

## The scorecard: six computable scores and one guardrail

| Variable | Definition | Better | Evidence | Computable from statements |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROIC including goodwill, level and 3-5 year trend | NOPAT / (equity + debt - cash) | Higher, stable or rising | Practitioner consensus; indirect academic | Yes |
| Organic growth | Revenue growth excluding acquisitions and FX | Higher | Practitioner consensus; weak academic | No, company disclosure |
| FCF per share growth | Multi-year CAGR of FCF / shares, in reporting currency | Higher | Leonard's preferred proxy; nets out dilution and interest | Yes |
| Net share issuance | Change in diluted share count over 3-5 years | Lower | Strong, replicated | Yes |
| Self-funded reinvestment | Acquisition spend / FCF, and share of spend covered by operating cash flow | High deployment, internally funded | Moderate to strong | Yes |
| Deal concentration and cadence | Largest deal or peak-year spend vs own history and market cap; steadiness of annual spend | Steady, no step-change | Strong for the tail; moderate for cadence | Partly; spend yes, deal list needs disclosure |
| Leverage (guardrail) | Net debt / EBITDA, leases treated consistently | Under a declared ceiling | Judgment; no settled evidence | Yes |

Leverage is a guardrail because theory and practice point in opposite directions and no large-sample result retrieved settles it. Declared ceilings from the acquirers themselves give anchors: Lifco caps net debt at 3x EBITDA ([Lifco](https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/lifco-interim-report-january-june-2026-20260714)) and WSP targets 1.0x to 2.0x ([WSP](https://www.wsp.com/en-gl/investors/press-releases/details/wsp-releases-strong-q4-2025-results-and-issues-2026-financial-outlook/3245102/2026)). Cash conversion and goodwill to total assets are worthwhile secondary flags where balance-sheet detail exists. Variables deliberately left out: raw deal count and asset growth (not supported as positives), private-target share (strongly supported but near-constant across this candidate set), acquirer size (supported but not a quality attribute), and valuation at the time of dealmaking (it would mechanically penalise the best-regarded compounders).

## Four sectors need four translations of the same variables

Reported net income is the most misleading generic input across all four sectors in 2025-26, because each company carried a large non-operating item. Constellation's FY2025 revenue rose 15% to $11,623M and FCFA2S rose 14% to $1,683M with organic growth of 4% ([Constellation](https://www.csisoftware.com/constellation-software-inc-announces-results-for-the-fourth-quarter-and-year-ended-december-31-2025-and-declares-quarterly-dividend/)), while net income fell about 30% ([Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stocks/ca/software/tsx-csu/constellation-software-shares/news/constellation-software-tsxcsu-is-down-148-after-revenue-rose)). Topicus's FCFA2S rose 23% to €218.7M while net income fell 53% on a €221.7M expense tied to its Asseco Poland stake ([Topicus](https://mfn.se/all/a/topicus-com-inc/constellation-software-inc-and-topicus-com-inc-announce-results-for-topicus-com-inc-for-the-fourth-quarter-and-year-ended-december-31-2025-0857b411)). The engine should therefore prefer cash-based per-share metrics computed uniformly from the cash-flow statement, since each company's own "free cash flow" deducts different items.

**Vertical market software.** Organic growth should be the FX-adjusted figure, which differs materially from reported (3% against 1% for Constellation in Q2 2026) ([Constellation](https://csisoftware.com/constellation-software-inc-announces-results-for-the-second-quarter-ended-june-30-2026-and-declares-quarterly-dividend/)). ROIC needs the most care here: asset-light economics inflate it as capital ages, so trend and FCF-per-share growth carry more weight than level. The deal-concentration variable is live for both names. Constellation lowered its hurdle rate for large deals in 2019 ([Constellation](https://www.csisoftware.com/category/press-releases/2019/05/02/constellation-software-inc.-announces-reduction-in-hurdle-rate-for-large-acquisitions)) and its founder stepped down as president in September 2025 ([The Logic](https://thelogic.co/briefing/constellation-software-founder-steps-down-citing-health-reasons/)); Topicus put €384.9M into a minority stake in Asseco, an atypical allocation that also makes single-period FCFA2S unreliable, as a €59.0M Asseco dividend showed in Q2 2026 ([Topicus](https://www.finanznachrichten.de/nachrichten-2026-08/69233522-constellation-software-inc-and-topicus-com-inc-announce-results-for-topicus-com-inc-for-the-second-quarter-ended-june-30-2026-399.htm)).

**Engineering consulting.** WSP's organic growth must be measured on net revenue, which strips pass-through subconsultant costs; it was 3.5% in FY2025. Leverage must be averaged or dated carefully: **0.9x at December 2025, flattered by equity raised ahead of the TRC deal, became 2.3x by June 2026** ([WSP Q4 2025](https://www.wsp.com/en-gl/investors/press-releases/details/wsp-releases-strong-q4-2025-results-and-issues-2026-financial-outlook/3245102/2026); [WSP Q2 2026](https://www.wsp.com/en-ca/news/2026/wsp-closes-q2-2026-with-accelerating-growth)). TRC, at US$3.3B in cash with roughly $850M of new equity and 14.5x forward EBITDA, is exactly the step-up in size and financing the concentration and issuance variables exist to catch ([Financier Worldwide](https://www.financierworldwide.com/fw-news/2025/12/18/wsp-global-strikes-33bn-trc-deal)). WSP's net debt also excludes leases, so the engine must apply one lease convention to all five.

**Trucking.** Profitability is the adjusted operating ratio, an inverse scale that must be sign-flipped, and single years mislead. TFI's ratio worsened to 91.9% from 89.9% in FY2025 while free cash flow rose to $832.3M ([TFI](https://tfiintl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/2/TFI-International-Press-Release-February-17-2026-EN.pdf)), then operating income rebounded 29% in Q2 2026 ([TFI](https://tfiintl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/7/TFI-International-Press-Release-July-27-2026-EN.pdf)). Organic growth is not a clean concept in a cyclical volume-and-price business, so ROIC and FCF per share should be through-cycle averages. TFI's buybacks (over $225M in 2025) score well on issuance; its integration record on its largest US deal is contested in the trade press ([FleetOwner](https://www.fleetowner.com/news/article/55237762/tfi-international-eyes-major-trucking-acquisition-focuses-on-ltl-growth)).

**Construction.** Backlog and awards stand in for organic growth, and contract type matters more than size. Aecon's backlog reached $10,714M in FY2025 while legacy fixed-price losses fell to CAD 94M from CAD 273M ([Aecon](https://www.daytraders.com/news/2026/03/05/aecon-reports-year-end-2025-results)). More fundamentally, its acquisitions are small bolt-ons that management calls "selective" ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/aecon-group-q4-earnings-call-220608250.html)), and no source reviewed names it as a serial acquirer. That is absence of evidence, not a verdict, but it means theme fit must be tested before any score is computed.

Margin levels are not comparable across these sectors, so the scorecard should never rank raw margins. Absolute per-share figures are not comparable either; growth rates and ratios computed within each company's reporting currency are.

## With five names, thresholds and tiers beat z-scores

Index providers turn scores into weights through a standard pipeline: winsorise, z-score against a parent universe of hundreds of stocks, average into a composite, then weight by score times market cap under caps ([MSCI](https://www.msci.com/eqb/methodology/meth_docs/MSCI_Quality_Indexes_Meth_Aug14.pdf); [S&P](https://spglobal.com/spdji/zh/documents/methodologies/methodology-sp-quality-indices.pdf)). That pipeline does not survive a five-name candidate set. With five observations the largest possible z-score is about 1.79, one unusual company defines the yardstick it is measured against, adding or dropping a candidate reshuffles every score, and across four sectors the z-score mostly measures sector. This is first-principles reasoning; no published source on small-sample z-scoring was found. Composite-indicator guidance does list categorical scales and rankings as legitimate alternatives and recommends testing more than one normalisation ([OECD/JRC](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/handbook-on-constructing-composite-indicators-methodology-and-user-guide_9789264043466-en.html)).

The defensible design has three steps. First, an **eligibility gate**, mirroring how thematic indexes screen for theme purity: is acquisition a stated, repeated strategy with material cumulative spend, and does the company clear the leverage ceiling? This step alone may leave four names. Second, **absolute threshold scoring**: each variable earns points against thresholds declared in advance and adapted by sector, summed to a composite. Published anchors exist for several: 12% goodwill-inclusive ROIC ([Aktagon](https://serial-acquirers.aktagon.com/scorecard/how-to-analyze/)), Halma's target of more than 5% organic growth and 90% cash conversion ([Halma](https://halma.com/news/press-releases/2025/full-year-results-2024-25)), and the leverage ceilings above. Threshold scores are stable to the candidate set and auditable. Third, a rank-average across the same variables as a sensitivity check; where the two orderings disagree, the disagreement is the finding to show.

For weights, **equal weight is the baseline: 3.0% to 3.6% each for five names in a 15-18% sleeve, or 3.75% to 4.5% each if the gate leaves four**. Morningstar's Wide Moat Focus index sets the precedent of screening, ranking and equal-weighting a conviction list ([Morningstar](https://indexes.morningstar.com/api/docs/65d830dbafcda3cfd78a12bc)). Where the scorecard produces clear separation, conviction tiers with fixed multiples of the equal weight are the coarsest honest tilt; continuous score-proportional weights over-read small gaps at this sample size. Guardrails should follow provider practice: a single-name cap and floor with proportional redistribution ([Solactive](https://solactive.com/downloads/Guideline-Solactive-SOLEBIZ.pdf)), and a combined cap on Constellation and Topicus, which are economically linked, in the spirit of Morningstar's 40% sector cap. Missing inputs should stay missing and reduce the maximum attainable score, never default to zero. Pure market-cap weighting would be dominated by Constellation and WSP and bears the least relation to the scorecard.

## Conclusion

The research inverts the intuitive definition of a serial acquirer. What marks a compounder is not the number of deals but a refusal to change pattern: the same deal size relative to itself, the same internal funding, a flat share count, and returns on total capital that hold as the capital base grows. Every one of the five candidates has a recent change of pattern on record, whether larger deals at lower hurdles, a minority stake, a part-equity-funded platform deal, or an appetite for "something of size". The scorecard's most valuable output is therefore the trend and step-change readings, not the levels.

Two implications follow for the build. The tool should present itself as a transparent rubric grounded in consensus, since no one, including the dedicated funds and screens, has published a tested model for this universe. And the hardest decision is upstream of the math: whether Aecon belongs in the theme at all is a human judgment the evidence leans against, and the weighting scheme should be designed to work with four names as readily as five.
